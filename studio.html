How we make them

One prompt. One film. No edit.

Most generative work is assembled: a dozen clips generated separately, then cut together. We don't work that way. Every episode is a single prompt and a single generation — the camera move, the cut inside the move, the sound cue, the reveal, all written into one piece of text and produced whole.

It is a harder way to work, and it is the reason the films hold together. The prompt is the film, so the craft lives there: we refine it until the whole thing lands in one piece, and that system gets sharper every day.

Format

Vertical 9:16 — the series is made for the phone.

Length

Up to 30 seconds, produced as one continuous piece.

Method

One prompt per film. No assembly in an editor.

Consistency

A locked character reference, so the same face carries across films.

What we’re building next

Generative film that holds an audience for minutes, not seconds.

Everything generated well today is short. A thirty-second film can carry one idea and one rule, and we have proved we can land that repeatedly. The thing nobody has made yet is longer — a piece where a character survives twenty cuts without drifting, where a world stays consistent across five minutes, where the sound was never recorded and you still believe it.

That is the work we are moving to, and it is the reason for this page. We are not short of ideas, budget or output. What moves the needle now is being closer to the frontier than a customer normally gets.

What a partnership looks like
  • —Early access. New models and features before they are public, so the work ships on the frontier rather than six months behind it.
  • —A line to the people building the tool. We work these systems to their limit every day and can say precisely where the frontier is.
  • —Flagship work. Films made to show what a tool can do at full stretch — not a demo reel, a piece people watch for its own sake.
  • —Reach that is already there. A channel with a real audience, plus our own publishing stack across Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and Facebook.

Canon already does a version of this with our founder. We are looking for the equivalent on the generative side — and if that is interesting, say so and we will show you what we are working on now.
